\section{Project Overview}\label{project-overview}

In this project I will use R to perform a basic data science workflow on
a customer transaction dataset. The project introduces the use of R for
data import, data inspection, data cleaning, exploratory data analysis
(EDA), visualization, and interpretation.

The dataset contains transaction-level information including customer
demographics, product characteristics, payment methods, transaction
dates, prices, quantities, ratings, and membership status.

\section{Key Findings The analysis shows that transaction spending is
right-skewed:
the}\label{key-findings-the-analysis-shows-that-transaction-spending-is-right-skewed-the}

mean (\textasciitilde\$803) is higher than the median
(\textasciitilde\$662), meaning most baskets are modest while a few
high-value purchases inflate the average. Home Appliances and
Electronics generate the most revenue, even though Clothing has the most
transactions, so revenue is driven by high-ticket categories rather than
frequency. UPI and Debit Card are the most frequently used payment
methods, while Credit Card baskets tend to be larger. Average basket
size is about three units, and total revenue is around \$240,935 across
the cleaned transactions. Membership status appears to matter: Premium
members spend the most per transaction and Basic members the least,
though the ANOVA p-value should be checked to confirm statistical
significance. Gender and ProductCategory are assessed using chi-square
and Cramér's V, while Age and Rating show weak linear relationships with
spending; Quantity and Price are the main drivers of TotalAmount. Sales
fluctuate by month, suggesting seasonality with mid-year and December
peaks. Finally, the cleaned data showed no impossible values and was
internally consistent, though duplicate handling and conflicting summary
figures should be verified before final reporting.

\section{8. Conclusions}\label{conclusions}

The R analysis reveals right-skewed spending, with a few large baskets
driving revenue while most purchases are modest. Home Appliances and
Electronics lead in revenue, whereas Clothing drives transaction
frequency. Digital payments dominate, and Premium members appear to
spend more per transaction. Age and product rating are weak predictors,
while category, quantity, price, and membership tier are stronger
signals. Sales show seasonal fluctuations. These findings offer clear
direction for product, payment, and retention strategy, though final
figures and significance tests should be verified before submission.
